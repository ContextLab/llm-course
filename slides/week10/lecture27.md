# Presentation format

<div class="note-box" data-title="What to cover in your presentation">

| Section | Time | Content |
|---------|------|---------|
| Problem statement | ~1 min | What question did you investigate? |
| Approach | ~1.5 min | What methods, models, or tools did you use? |
| Results | ~1.5 min | What did you find? Show key figures or outputs. |
| Reflection | ~1 min | What worked? What surprised you? What would you do differently? |

</div>

<div class="tip-box" data-title="Tips for a great presentation">

- **Show, don't tell**: Demos and visualizations are more compelling than bullet points
- **Be honest**: Negative results and failed approaches are valuable and interesting
- **Connect to course themes**: How does your project relate to concepts we covered?
- **Keep it concise**: 5 minutes goes fast -- practice timing beforehand

</div>

# What we have learned

<div class="important-box" data-title="The big ideas">

1. **Language models are compression**: They compress the statistical structure of language into learnable parameters
2. **Scale unlocks capabilities**: Emergent abilities appear at larger model sizes (few-shot learning, reasoning, code generation)
3. **Architecture matters, but data matters more**: The transformer is powerful, but training data quality and quantity drive performance
4. **Alignment is unsolved**: Making models helpful, honest, and harmless remains an open research problem
5. **The field moves fast**: Many concepts we covered were published in the last 2--5 years

</div>

---

# What comes next

<div class="note-box" data-title="Frontiers in language modeling (2025 and beyond)">

**Reasoning models**: Systems like o1 and o3 that "think" step-by-step before answering, producing stronger results on math, science, and logic problems.

**Multimodal integration**: Models that natively process text, images, audio, and video together -- understanding the world through multiple senses simultaneously.

**Longer contexts and better memory**: Context windows growing to millions of tokens, plus persistent memory across conversations.

**Agentic AI**: Models that can plan, use tools, write code, and take real-world actions autonomously -- the focus of Lecture 24.

**Efficient architectures**: State space models, mixture of experts, and new attention variants that reduce compute requirements while maintaining quality.

</div>
